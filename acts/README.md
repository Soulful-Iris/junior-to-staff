# The acts

An act is a tier. Three of them, five projects each, taken after the sections in
that tier.

| act | the question | the five |
|---|---|---|
| [Act 1 · Junior](act-1-junior/) | can you build the thing at all? | a reading list, a receipt tracker, a shift schedule, a link-rot watcher, a public form |
| [Act 2 · Senior](act-2-senior/) | can it survive load, failure and a bad deploy? | degrade don't stop, twenty deploys a day, debuggable at 3am, the flood, an AI feature you can defend |
| [Act 3 · Staff](act-3-staff/) | can you change what gets built? | the migration you finish, the strategy you found, the incident you caused, the paved road, the thing you decided not to build |

Pick one per act. They are alternatives, not a sequence — five different domains
so you can choose the one whose hard part you least want to do, which is where
the learning is.

The three acts shift in kind as they go. In Act 1 the deliverable is a system.
In Act 2 it is the same system, survivable, with nothing added that a user can
see. In Act 3 it is usually a document, a deletion, or somebody else's changed
situation — and that is not a softening of the material, it is the job.

Smaller than these: the five projects beside each section, an afternoon each.
Larger and continuous: [the spine](../projects/), one system all the way
through. How all of them are written: [docs/PROJECT-SPEC.md](../docs/PROJECT-SPEC.md).
