# CrowdStrike track

<section class="chapter-context" markdown="1">

## One company, one loop, in its own league

This track sits outside the core book and after the company studio; it prepares one senior cloud backend loop at CrowdStrike and nothing else. It is built from three kinds of evidence, labeled everywhere they appear: the live job posting and the company's engineering blog `[Official]`, dated candidate reports `[Reported]`, and drills written for this track in the reported shapes `[Generated]`.

Preparation comes first: the rounds, who grades what, the reported questions, the values, a Go refresher for a Python interviewer, and a two-week plan. Then the coding problems, each a full bundle with a contract, six settled scenarios, a worked lesson, a reference implementation, and tests. Then the architecture chapter: how CrowdStrike builds its platform at a trillion events a day, and the design cases their reviews ask, each worked with diagrams and follow-ups. Last, the take-home and the review, the two rounds that decide the level: the document with a complete example, every reported follow-up with its answer shape, a rehearsed two-hour transcript, and a week of drills.

![The CrowdStrike senior loop as a pipeline: recruiter screen, hiring manager call, live coding, design take-home, ninety-minute review, code review, collaboration panel, decision](../../assets/crowdstrike/loop-pipeline.svg)

Read the preparation chapter once, end to end. Then alternate: one coding bundle and one architecture case per day, each finished with its changed requirement. The plan lesson at the end of T1 sequences the fourteen days; T4's drills take over the last seven days before the review.

**Evidence policy.** Sources were checked on 25 September 2026. Candidate reports span 2019 to 2026 and are anecdotes, not transcripts; aggregator sites copy each other, so agreement between them is not confirmation. Nothing here is claimed to be "most asked." A recruiter's description of your loop overrides this track.
