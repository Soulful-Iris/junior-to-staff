Researched pedagogy for the engineering guide (subagent). What held up: retrieval practice (g~0.5-0.6, Adesope 2017; weaker for math per 2025 meta), spacing (robust, but "optimal gap" numbers are lab fact-learning: ~10-20% of retention interval, Cepeda 2008), worked examples for novices with expertise reversal (Kalyuga) so scaffolding must fade, seductive-details/coherence (Rey 2012; worst next to relevant diagrams). Contested: germane cognitive load (unfalsifiability, de Jong 2010), interleaving beyond procedural math (failed replications 2025 JRME), deliberate practice variance (Macnamara vs Ericsson), productive failure (real but conceptual-knowledge-only; needs consolidation phase). Dead: learning styles meshing (Pashler 2008), learning-pyramid percentages (fabricated - Thalheimer/Letrud).
Structure models that work: Nand2Tetris = spec + supplied tests per module, artifact feeds next module, "done" is external and ungameable (SIGCSE 2009, CACM 2023). Crafting Interpreters = every line in the book, runnable artifact every chapter, code written+tested before prose. Odin Project = projects interleaved, official docs as reading. Execute Program = enforced spacing, can't binge. Wilson Ten Quick Tips = backward design: write the final assessment first.
Roadmap critique evidence: HN 21591441 ("bags of words", no prioritization, "know 100 things to be at the bottom of the food chain"); MOOC completion 3.13%, 52% never start (Reich, Science 2019). Guides die because they are recognition lists, not schedules of work with checks.
Writing that survives: Diataxis (don't mix tutorial/reference/explanation modes), jvns 13 confusing-explanation patterns (concrete first, one concept at a time, why not just what), gwern Long Content (living documents, versioned, maintained). Diagrams: C4 - one abstraction level per diagram; diagram only stable levels, lower levels churn and go stale.
